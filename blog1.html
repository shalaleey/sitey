Breaking the Ice

1st Face-to-Face Class

Last Saturday, we officially began our first face-to-face session in Advanced Technology Project Management, and it turned out to be a meaningful and eye-opening experience. Having interacted only through virtual meetings before, meeting my classmates in person created a sense of anticipation and connection that felt different from online learning.

The day started with introductions. Hearing about my classmates’ diverse backgrounds and interests gave me an appreciation for the variety of perspectives we bring into this subject. In project management, collaboration is key, and I realized that this diversity will be an advantage as we learn to approach problems from different angles.

Next, we established classroom rules and policies. This may seem simple, but I found it important because clear expectations create a respectful and productive learning environment. Just like in managing a project, setting ground rules ensures that everyone is on the same page, roles are defined, and communication flows smoothly.

The instructor then introduced the course overview and objectives. What excited me most was the focus on hands-on projects and collaborative activities. Advanced Technology Project Management is not just about theories—it’s about applying principles to real-world scenarios, making decisions under constraints, and finding creative solutions to challenges. I could already see how the subject would push us to grow in both knowledge and skills.

The highlight of the class was the nine-dots activity. At first, the task of connecting nine dots with only four straight lines felt impossible. But once I realized that the solution required thinking outside the box, it clicked. This activity reflected the essence of project management: sometimes the solutions we need are not within the boundaries we initially set, and true innovation comes when we are willing to explore beyond the usual approach.

Looking back on our first session, I see how it laid the foundation for the weeks ahead. The introductions helped build connections, the rules established structure, the course overview clarified our path, and the activity sparked a valuable lesson about creativity and problem-solving.

This reflection leaves me excited for what’s to come. I look forward to learning how to manage technology-driven projects more effectively while strengthening collaboration with my classmates. Most importantly, I carry with me the reminder that successful project management often begins when we dare to think differently.
