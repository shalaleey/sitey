Project Foundations

Second Face-to-Face Session

Our second session in Advanced Technology Project Management focused on understanding the basics of managing projects and the roles that guide their success. We discussed why projects fail and the importance of proper planning, highlighted by the facilitator’s question: “Do projects fail because you fail to plan or you plan to fail?” This question set the tone for the day and made me reflect on the importance of preparation in every step of a project.

We began with a glimpse of the university website project, an example that illustrated the complexity of real-life projects. The discussion emphasized that project management is not just about completing tasks; it is about organizing resources, managing time, and ensuring quality outcomes. I learned that IT projects combine hardware and software, while information systems also include peopleware, emphasizing the human aspect of technology projects.

Leadership roles were another key topic. We explored six leadership types and various managerial responsibilities required to guide a project effectively. Understanding these roles gave me clarity on how teams function and why assigning clear responsibilities is crucial for achieving objectives.

We then moved on to defining what a project truly is and how project planning works. Planning, redirecting, and organizing are essential phases, and each affects the others. This reminded me of the project triangle—cost, time, and scope—and how adjusting one aspect impacts the rest. The discussion highlighted the importance of balancing these elements to achieve successful outcomes.

Finally, we examined what makes a project successful. Clear objectives, careful planning, proper resource allocation, and effective communication all play a role. I realized that success is not only completing tasks but also delivering value and ensuring that stakeholders’ expectations are met.

Looking back, the session gave me a solid foundation in project management concepts and the mindset required to approach tasks systematically. I feel more prepared to apply these ideas in practical situations and appreciate the planning and effort required to ensure project success.

This reflection leaves me motivated to approach future projects with more structure and understanding. I recognize that learning the principles now will help me manage real-world challenges more effectively and strengthen collaboration with my peers.
